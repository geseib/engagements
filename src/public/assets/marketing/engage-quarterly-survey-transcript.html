Quarterly meeting survey walkthrough

Northstar Studio, Project Beacon and the teammates are fictional. These are real screens from demo 6584.

A meeting ends. What should change next time? Northstar Studio asks its five teammates for feedback on their quarterly meeting. A short survey gives each person a way to answer before the group moves on. Here is how the host turns those answers into a useful record.

Maya creates a survey with five questions. She asks for a rating, a useful part of the meeting, clearer next steps, a ranked priority and one practical change. She opens it and shares the session code. People join on their own devices, with no account to create.

Priya gives the meeting five out of five. She chooses the customer discussion as the most useful part. Each answer saves as she goes. This survey is anonymous: the host sees the totals and written feedback, without names attached to those answers.

The questions can ask for more than a score. Priya ranks customer evidence first for next quarter, then adds a written suggestion. Another teammate says the next steps are not clear yet and explains why: who owns the first prototype, and when will the team review it?

Priya checks her answers and sends them. She can still make a change until the host closes the survey. Back on the main screen, Maya sees five out of five finished. She closes the survey so the answers are fixed and the team can read the results together.

The result visuals make the pattern clear. The average rating is four out of five. Customer discussion was the most picked part. But only three people say the next steps are clear. One says not yet, and one is unsure. A good overall rating still leaves a problem to solve.

The ranked results put customer evidence first. Written feedback asks for an earlier brief, shorter updates, more time to talk, and clear owners and dates. These are concrete changes the host can use when planning the next meeting. Read the words as well as the charts.

Maya opens the session report. The results and written responses stay together in one place. She saves it for one year and downloads a copy. Use a survey after a meeting, a workshop or a training session. Ask what helped, what was unclear, and what should happen next.
