Call and Answer walkthrough

Northstar Studio, Project Beacon and the five teammates are fictional. These are real screens from demo session 8861.

A new team does not need all the answers on day one. It needs a way to hear everyone. Meet Northstar Studio, a fictional tech company building Project Beacon. Maya manages the team. Priya leads product. Eli and Ben are engineers. Jules is the architect. Their first customers, their platform and their way of working are still open questions.

Maya creates a Call and Answer engagement. She gives it a title, chooses the team’s custom questions and adds a short project brief for Workie. The team has six weeks to learn and test one useful idea. These details give the discussion a clear starting point. Then she opens the room.

People scan the code or open the join link and type their name. No account is needed. Priya joins from her laptop. Jules uses a tablet. A laptop or tablet works best here: there is more typing than in trivia or a survey. Back on the host screen, all five teammates are ready.

The first question asks who Beacon should learn from first, and why. Each person writes an answer on their own screen. Priya wants to speak with small software teams. Jules suggests internal platform teams. Different roles see different risks. Everyone gets time to put a clear idea into words before the room compares them.

Maya opens voting. The room reads the answers without names attached, so the ideas can stand on their own. Each teammate ranks their first, second and third choices. Priya makes her picks and submits them. The host can see when all five votes are in, then reveal the result.

The results show which ideas drew support. Workie reads the responses in light of the project brief and pulls out points for the team to discuss. This is a starting point for judgment, not a decision made for them. Maya opens a feedback round so people can add what is missing or challenge an assumption.

Priya adds a concrete follow-up: ask customers to show the last request that got lost. Jules flags a risk: a useful internal tool may not be a product someone will buy. Their feedback stays with the round. The team then moves on to a small experiment and a working agreement. They are shaping a plan together.

After the discussion, Maya opens the session report. The questions, responses, votes and feedback give the team a record to return to. She saves the report and downloads a copy. Start with your own questions and the situation your team faces. A new platform, a hard choice or a better way to work. What will you build?
