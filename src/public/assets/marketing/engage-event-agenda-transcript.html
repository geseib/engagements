Event agenda walkthrough

Northstar Studio, Project Beacon and the teammates are fictional. The app views are real screens from demo event 9630. The welcome presentation is shown as labeled prepared PDF slides; this first cut does not demonstrate a PDF upload or live slide navigation.

A team day should feel like one event. Northstar Studio is bringing its new Beacon team together. Maya plans a welcome, a quick warm-up, two working discussions and time to build. People join once. The host guides them through the day.

Maya gives each item a title, a length and a presenter. Presentations, trivia, Call and Answer, surveys and offline activities can sit in the same agenda. Breaks have a place too. The plan gives the team a clear order without forcing every activity onto a screen.

The welcome sets the scene. Beacon is still an idea. The team needs to learn who it should help, which problem matters and what to test first. Maya uses a short presentation to frame the day. Participants can see which agenda item is live.

Next, Maya starts the eighties trivia warm-up. Priya stays in the same event and her screen follows the host into the game. She answers a quick music question. It gives the team a light start before they turn to the harder questions about their new project.

Maya opens Direction Builder. The team moves from a quick answer to a thoughtful one. Priya suggests starting with small software product teams and asking about the last customer request they lost. A laptop or tablet works best here: people need room to write, read and respond.

A break is part of the event too. Maya starts it from the agenda. Priya sees the break and a return time on her screen. No one needs a new link or code. When the team is ready, the host chooses the next engagement and brings everyone back together.

For new ideas, Maya starts Lessons from the School of Hard Books. A lesson from medicine asks the team to do no harm. Priya proposes trying sample data before changing a customer’s tools. The same event can make room for a warm-up, a focused discussion and a fresh way to think.

The rest of the agenda leaves time for prototype planning, meeting feedback and next steps. Maya decides what goes live and when. Throughout the day, the team keeps the same event code: nine, six, three, zero. Plan the flow, invite your people and guide the next useful conversation.
