School of Hard Books walkthrough

Northstar Studio, Project Beacon and the teammates are fictional. These are real screens from demo 7545.

A team can get stuck asking the same questions. A lesson from another field can open a different door. Northstar Studio is building Project Beacon. Its five teammates have six weeks to learn who needs it, what to build, and how to work together.

Maya chooses Lessons from the School of Hard Books. She gives the session a name and adds the project brief. The team will borrow ideas from architecture, cooking and design. The lesson starts the conversation. Their own situation gives it a purpose.

The first lesson is about thresholds: the space between one state and another. How could that help a new Beacon customer? Priya suggests trying three sample requests before connecting real tools. Other teammates propose a sandbox, a small import, and clear boundaries between experiments and production.

Everyone has time to write. Then Maya opens voting. The team reads the ideas without names and picks a first, second and third choice. When all five votes are in, she reveals the results. The votes show which ideas drew support. They give the discussion a place to begin.

Workie reads the answers in light of the brief and draws out points to discuss. Priya adds a useful check: a smooth demo does not prove customers need Beacon. She will test the sample workspace with two product leads and record where they hesitate. That feedback stays with the round.

Next comes a lesson from cooking: taste as you go. The team proposes weekly customer tests and an experiment log. A design lesson asks what they can remove. Their answers narrow the first prototype to one customer group, one painful workflow and one clear path to a decision.

Maya opens Rounds, then the session report. All three lessons are here, with the responses, votes, analysis and follow-up comments. The team can trace an idea back to the question that prompted it. She saves the report for one year and downloads a copy.

Use these lessons to explore an opportunity, challenge a habit or find a better question. Your team supplies the ideas and decides what to test. What could a different field help you see?
