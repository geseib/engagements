School of Architecture

The importance of thresholds

Architect Christopher Alexander taught that thresholds, the spaces between inside and outside, are crucial for human comfort. A good threshold gives a gradual transition rather than an abrupt change. That holds for buildings, for user interfaces and for organizational boundaries. When you design any system, build in buffer zones that help people adjust instead of forcing a sudden shift.

Your turn Describe how you apply thresholds in your work: a gradual transition where there would otherwise be an abrupt one.

Planning

If an agent can build it from the ticket, who writes the ticket?

When the distance from a written intent to a running branch collapses to minutes, the written intent becomes the expensive part. Three positions are all defensible. One: specs get more rigorous than ever, because ambiguity now produces confident wrong code instead of a question in standup. Two: specs get cheaper and more disposable, because when three implementations cost an afternoon you stop arguing and go look. Three: the ticket stops existing and the branch becomes the proposal.

Your turn Which way is your team actually drifting, and is that good? Two or three sentences.

5 · Results (Room)

The takeaway is the winning answer, read large in full. The runners-up keep their text. The bar is the fill behind each answer, relative to the winner, so the result and what was voted on are one thing. There are no vote counts or percentages: in a round where names are hidden, a number beside an answer lets its author be worked out (the 16 Oct rule). In a named round the credit line and points come back. The bottom line gives the host a way into the discussion that doesn't ask anyone to own up.
