Why a row of tabs, and not a segmented control or a dropdown

OptionForAgainstVerdict
Row of tabs, with counts RecommendedEvery place is named on screen, and the count on a tab you are not on tells you to look there: that is the owner's ask ("list at the top all of your teams"). One click, no menu. Works for the common case (Personal and one team) at a glance. Overflow folds into N more, which keeps order and counts.Needs a 46px row. A ninth team lives in a menu. At phone width it has to become a button and a sheet.Use it. It replaces the chip; still one switcher.
Segmented controlCompact; reads as "one of these", which is right.Breaks past three or four choices, and team names are long ("Bayview Hospital Training"). Segments of equal width either truncate every name or grow the row. Counts inside segments get cramped.No.
Dropdown (today's chip, OrgSwitcher.jsx)Scales to any number. Already built and tested.Hides the other places and anything waiting in them. A team owner with three people waiting in another team cannot see it without opening a menu they have no reason to open. Today's menu also carries Create and Act as Engage, so its job is already three jobs.Keep only as the phone sheet and the More menu.
Tabs AND the chipNo code removed.Two controls saying "who am I right now". The tenancy work spent two rounds removing exactly this kind of doubling (consoleSections.js:41-61).No.

Open questions (detail and recommendations in SPEC.md §9)

  1. May a team owner or admin approve hosting for people who accepted their invitation? Today only Engage staff move an account from pending to hosts. Recommended: yes, for accounts that joined through that team's invitation; Engage is told and can still refuse.
  2. Counts on every tab need one new read. Recommended: GET /me/attention returns a number per scope; the boxes on the open tab come from routes that already exist.
  3. Name the section "Overview". Recommended, and make it the first item and the landing section in every scope (it moves Organisations to second for staff).
  4. Alerts above the numbers, or the numbers first? Recommended: alerts first.
  5. Should a box ever be dismissed? Recommended: no. A box goes when its cause goes. Information boxes (staff opened your content) clear when you open the log.
  6. The console on a phone. Recommended: Overview and the scope sheet only at first; other sections say "Open on a laptop" below 760px.
  7. Plan requests: decide inline or in the dialog? Recommended: Decide opens the existing dialog, which requires the note the customer reads.
  8. Moderation: approve from the Overview? Recommended: no. Review opens the item; a set is judged on its findings.
