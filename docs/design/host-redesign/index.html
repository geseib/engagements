Revision 2. Mockups for docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-08-host-screen-redesign-design.md, revised against CRITIQUE.md. One stage, four display profiles, no scrolling and no silent clipping in any state.

Revision 3. Three people who run meetings for a living reviewed revision 2 and two of them would not have run it. The headline fix: line clamps used to live in the base CSS, so content reached the fitter pre-truncated and it stopped early — five of six trivia options cut mid-word with 131px of empty space beneath them. Truncation is now the fitter's last act, and audit checks A10 and A11 fail if a reduction fires before space actually runs out.
These carry worst-case content on purpose. The longest plausible question, six long trivia options, forty players, sixty distinct wavelength terms, a 900-word model response. The previous revision used copy chosen to fit, which is why sixteen green pages hid nine defects. Where a state cannot hold its worst case you should see the reduction fire — smaller type, a different column count, the meter's column taken, or a line saying what moved to the report. What you should never see is text cut off — and never while space sits empty beside it.
How to judge them. Type is sized in vh with pixel floors, so a mockup only shows its true size at the true display size. Open one, press F11 (or ⌃⌘F), and where you can, put it on the real projector. Switch profile in the viewer with 1–4, or by editing the class on <html> — d-room, d-tv, d-call, d-table — if you have a single page open on its own.

One thing that looks like a bug and is not: on a viewport under 820px tall, TV renders at Room's sizes. That is a deliberate @media (max-height:820px) fallback — the TV ladder cannot be honoured on a short viewport without clipping the trivia states, so it reverts rather than protecting a number. Judge TV at 900px or more.

Verification

audit.html runs every mockup at four profiles × two viewports, plus a wide-face stress pass approximating Archivo Expanded, and asserts nine properties. Three of them exist because the first sweep was structurally incapable of failing: A2 silent clipping, A5 a display profile that renders identically to another, A6 an overlay covering the primary action. Every check was demonstrated failing against the pre-critique files before being trusted — press Run against _baseline/ to see that for yourself.

Run the audit with the tab in the foreground. Chrome throttles timers in background tabs and the harness will appear to hang.

States

  1. STARTED / lobby

    Longest event title, 40 players. One QR, one code, one meter — the roster is a dot matrix because forty names cannot be read from the back row and are not the question.

  2. ASK · Call & Answer

    The longest prompt a strategic session plausibly produces. The dock names who we are waiting on.

  3. ASK · Trivia

    Six long options — the case that decapitated the question in the previous revision. Watch the fitter step down instead.

  4. ASK · Wavelength

    The subject owns the screen. Meter says ANSWERED, matching the dock.

  5. VOTE (anonymous)

    Answers carry stable numbers, not names. Anonymity is stated once in the kicker rather than on every card.

  6. RESULTS before the reveal

    Ranked on merit with no authors and no standings — a live leaderboard would be attribution by arithmetic.

  7. RESULTS · Trivia

    Recap, six-option distribution, correct row carrying the headline weight, standings in the meter.

  8. RESULTS · Wavelength

    Sixty distinct terms, top sixteen shown as a ranked weighted flow, the reduction stated on screen. No packed cloud, no collisions.

  9. RESULTS · Field Notes beat

    A 900-word Workie response reduced to one headline and three prompts, two columns, nothing to scroll.

  10. ENDED

    Champion stated once. Join line replaced by the session summary — a finished session must not still be inviting people in.

  11. Console overlay

    The drawer stops at the dock, so the primary action stays visible and clickable while it is open. Close button, no email address.

  12. Profile: Table

    Laptop as the display. Its own ladder with a 16px angular floor — not the Room ladder times a multiplier.

  13. Profile: Call

    Screen-share. Keeps Room’s type on purpose (the encoder is the constraint, not the eye); flat field, 2px rules.

  14. Profile: Large TV

    A 65-inch panel at ~30 ppi needs a bigger ladder than a projected image. Less content fits — the reduction firing here is the design working.

  15. Edge: one player, no answers

    The under-filled case, an artwork round, and a disabled primary that explains itself in one line.

  16. Transition: phase wipe

    Four simultaneous signals on every phase change — the one thing worth stating more than once. Unanimously the strongest screen in first-look testing; unchanged.

  17. Companion: the phone remote

    Optional, never required — and the only surface that may hold a name or a correct answer. Two screens: driving the round, and browsing questions.

  18. Setup: choose the next question

    Scan, filter and commit mid-session. Correct answers are absent rather than hidden, because the stage is a shared surface in every profile.

  19. Lobby beat: how this works

    The player instructions, rewritten from ~180 words to four lines and put on the stage where players can actually read them.

  20. Setup: anonymous responses

    The new option in context, default on, with the guarantee and its limits written out. Not a stage; sized for arm-length reading.

  21. RESULTS after the reveal

    Authorship annotated in place and the standings unfrozen. Compare with 06, which is the same round before the host reveals.

Colours follow docs/handoff/warm-summit-design-spec.md. The files are self-contained — no CDN, no external assets — so display type falls back to the nearest system face; production self-hosts Archivo Expanded, which is materially wider, and the audit’s A8 pass simulates that penalty with +5.5% tracking.
