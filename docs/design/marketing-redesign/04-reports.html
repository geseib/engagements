The summit

What a session leaves behind.

The report is written as the session runs. This is a sample from an invented session, with the six things it captures marked.

Session report

Q3 planning — what we stop, what we start

Thursday 18 September · 20 participants · 6 questions · code 4821

1Question 3 of 6 · call and answer

What should we stop doing in the next quarter?

  • 2Parallel discovery on three products at once 39 votes Priya N. · “We are three deep on everything and finished on nothing.”
  • The weekly status meeting nobody reads 7 votes 4Tomas B. · comment from Jo R.: “Four of us do read it — keep it, shorten it.”
  • Hand-built release notes 4 votes Alina K. · “Forty minutes a week, every week.”
  • Two-week estimates on unscoped work 0 votes Sam O.

5Summary, discussion and next steps

The room agreed on one thing far more strongly than on anything else: too much discovery is running at once. Two of the four answers describe recurring process cost rather than product work, which suggests the quarter’s real constraint is attention, not capacity.

Questions worth taking further:

  1. If only one product could be in discovery next quarter, which one, and what happens to the other two?
  2. The status meeting drew both a stop vote and a defence. Who is it for?

Next steps:

  1. Name one product as the discovery focus before the next planning session.
  2. Decide who owns release notes, or decide to stop writing them.

Getting it out

Export and sharing

PDF

The whole report as one file, laid out for reading rather than for a browser window. The file is yours to circulate however you circulate things.

Print

A print sheet for the people who want it on the table at the next meeting.

A saved link

A link to the report, kept for a set time or kept permanently — you choose which when you save it.

Anonymity

Names can be hidden for a whole session.

Set it when you create the session. Answers, votes and comments are then recorded without the name attached, on the screen and in the report.

It is a per-session choice, not a per-answer one, so nobody has to decide in the moment whether this particular sentence is safe to sign. A session that starts anonymous stays anonymous.

Run one session. Read the report. Decide from there.

Design notes — 04 reports
The pins are in the sheet, the text is outside itAnnotation laid over the thing it annotates is how the tenancy set hid four table rows. The numbered markers sit in the paper; the sentences live in their own column.
Amber carries a pin, never a word#F6A94C is 1.84:1 on the paper. The pin is a #0F1A2E numeral on amber (8.9:1); every other warm thing on this sheet is --mk-amber-ink.
Callout 3 is the honesty pinThe report has votes. It has no rating, no score for a call-and-answer round, and no preference field. This page is where the wording gets settled for every other page.
Callout 6 explains its own absenceStandings come from the trivia rounds only. Saying why is shorter than the question it prevents.
Anonymity is a section, not a footnoteIt is the first thing a facilitator asks about a retrospective tool, so it gets a heading rather than a line in a feature list.
