Base camp

Turn your team’s own material into decisions everyone climbed toward.

Build question sets from what your team already has. Run them as trivia to warm the room up, or as call and answer to collect every idea and put it to a vote. Everyone plays from their phone. The session ends with a report.

Two ways to play

One set of questions, two shapes of round.

Both run on the screen at the front of the room while everyone answers on their own phone.

Trivia

Warm the room up, or check what landed.

A question, four options, one right answer. The room answers, the answer is revealed with its explanation, and the standings move.

  • Ask, then results. Trivia has no vote phase.
  • Each question carries its own category, difficulty and explanation.
  • Running standings after every question.
Call and answer

Pose a prompt, collect every idea, then vote.

Everyone writes at once, so the room hears from the people it usually does not. Then the room reads the answers and votes on them.

  • Ask, vote, results — three phases you move through.
  • Every answer is kept, not only the ones that won votes.
  • The vote breakdown is kept per answer and goes into the report.

Your own material

The questions come from your work, not from a quiz pack.

Hand the builder the strategy document, the retro notes, the deck you are about to present. It drafts a set. You decide what runs.

1

Supply the material

Documents and topics you already have. Or write the questions yourself — the builder is a convenience, not a requirement.

2

A set is drafted

The AI builders turn it into trivia questions or call-and-answer prompts, each with its category and its detail.

3

You review and edit

Preview the set as a player will see it, change anything, drop anything. Nothing runs until you start a session with it.

4

It is stored where it belongs

In your organisation’s private library, encrypted. Or take a starting point from the moderated public library.

Private stays private. An organisation’s sets are encrypted and are readable only inside that organisation. The public library is separate, moderated, and nothing reaches it without being published on purpose.

Answers arrive live. The team votes. The strongest ideas rise.

The answer with no votes is kept too. A session that quietly discards it is a session you cannot go back to.

Everyone writes at the same time, so the room does not have to take turns to be heard.

  • Answers appear on the front screen as they are submitted.
  • The room reads them, then votes — on what was actually said, not on who said it loudest.
  • The count is a vote count. It is not a quality score and the report never calls it one.
  • Names can be hidden for a whole session if the subject needs it.

The summit

Everyone leaves with the same page.

The report is written as the session runs. Nobody has to type the whiteboard up afterwards.

Summary and next steps

The room agreed on one thing far more strongly than on anything else: too much discovery is running at once. Two of the four answers describe recurring process cost rather than product work, which suggests the quarter’s real constraint is attention, not capacity.

  1. Name one product as the discovery focus before the next planning session.
  2. Decide who owns release notes, or decide to stop writing them.
  3. Revisit the status meeting with the four people who said they read it.

Design notes — 01 home
The hero is a place, not a bannerThe scene is position:fixed, so the sections wash over the same mountain rather than scrolling past a picture. The route and the climber are the page’s own spine: base camp at the hero, the flag at the report.
Why the glow stops where it doesThe art brief measures #F4EDE4 at 2.84:1 on #C77B4A. So the amber is boxed into the bottom 40% of the hero band and the headline lives in the top 55%. Nothing warm may rise into it.
Join stays in the hero/ becomes marketing, so the four-digit entry had to go somewhere a player arriving mid-session can find it in one look. The full page survives at /join; this is the same component at variant="compact".
Only --mk-text inside the join cardThe card is rgba(27,41,66,0.82) over the glow. Composited on pure amber that leaves --mk-muted at 4.26:1 — under AA. So the label and the hint are full-strength warm white.
The stills are drawn, not photographedEvery clip slot is plain divs: no image file, no other origin. Sub-project 2 drops a muted looping clip behind the same frame; the drawing stays as the poster.
The zero-vote answer is on purposeAn answer nobody voted for is still in the tally and still in the report. The count is described as a vote count everywhere it appears, and never as a rating or a preference.
Trivia has no vote phaseIts list says “ask, then results” because saying otherwise is the easiest untrue sentence to write about this product.
The paper sheetMarkup and data-theme="light" convert in the same change — dusk copy on paper measures 1.2:1. Amber draws the meter bars and never carries text here; --mk-amber-ink says the numbers.
