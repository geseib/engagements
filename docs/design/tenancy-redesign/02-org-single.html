Design notes
The switcher is in the topbar, not the navThe left nav lists places inside one organisation. The switcher changes which organisation those places belong to — a different axis, so a different spot. It sits beside the environment chip because both answer the same question: which world am I looking at?
One org, no menuA user who belongs to a single organisation gets the same chip without a caret and without a menu. A control whose menu has one item teaches people to ignore the control.
Two numbers that look wrong together, and are notThe nav says 3 and the billing meter says 3 of 5, but this list has five rows. Sets from the Engage library and the public library are readable by every team and stored by none, so they cost nothing and are not counted. An earlier cut said “12” in the nav against “2 of 5” on the invoice with nothing explaining the gap — which reads as a bug in the bill.
Whose, before whatThe second column is what tells the free rows from the billed ones at a glance. Without it, the only way to know whether a set counts against your plan is to open it.
