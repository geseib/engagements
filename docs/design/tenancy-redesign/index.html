Multi-tenant Engage

Twelve screens covering the whole of what changes when Engage stops being one shared environment and becomes a place where separate organisations keep their own work. Press N on any page to hide the design notes — that is the state to judge the screen in.

The worked example throughout is Northwind Learning, a training outfit of three on the $5 Team plan. In the month shown they ran 20 sessions and stored 3 sets, so their bill is $5 + $0 + $3.75 = $8.75.

Everyone also has a space of their own. A personal organisation is created with the account, is free for 5 sessions and 5 stored sets a month, and becomes a $5 Team the moment you invite somebody. There is no "belongs to no organisation" state after approval — which is the point: it was a state every handler had to remember to consider, and every place that forgot was a bug.

The organisation dimension

Which team am I working in, and how do I move between them?

01 Switching organisation The switcher open. Three memberships with a different role in each, and the question sets underneath belong to exactly one of them. 02 One organisation, no menu The same chip for somebody who belongs to a single team. No caret, no menu — a control whose menu has one item teaches people to ignore it. 09 Approved, but in no team yet Join the invitation you were sent, or start your own. Two doors, not a wizard. The nav is empty because there is nowhere to go yet.

Running a team

Who is in it, what it costs, and what happens to the data.

03 Members and invitations Two lists because they are two situations. An invitation that expires in three days says so. The last owner cannot be demoted, and the row says why. 04 Plan & usage The usage meter, past the included allowance. The invoice is shown as four lines of arithmetic, and nothing is ever blocked mid-session. 12 Your own space, at its limit A personal org is free for 5 sessions and 5 sets, then needs a team. The gate lands on creating a session, never on one already running in front of a room. 08 Data & privacy What is encrypted, every time anyone at Engage read anything and why, and a one-click export that needs no conversation.

Sharing, and the review that gates it

Nothing leaves a team without a check, and a rejection has to be actionable.

05 Sharing a set publicly What the automated review actually looks for, all three outcomes stated up front, and the fact that publishing makes an independent copy. 06 A set that needs changes Two of thirty questions flagged, quoted, each with a reason that separates the topic from the treatment — plus a route to a human. 07 The public library Browse what other teams published and copy it. Your own published sets appear here too, listed exactly as everyone else sees them.

The platform side

What somebody who works on Engage sees — and, more importantly, what they do not.

Engage staff 10 Organisations Accounts, plans and billing. There is no “view their sets” button, and the left nav has no content section at all. Reading a customer’s data takes a request with a reason that expires in four hours. Engage staff 11 Moderation queue The sets the automated check would not decide alone. The one screen where staff read customer content by design — and it is still logged.
